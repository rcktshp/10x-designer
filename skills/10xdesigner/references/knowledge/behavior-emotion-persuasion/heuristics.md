# Heuristics — Behavior, Emotion & Persuasion

Deduplicated "if X then problem Y, fix Z" critique rules.

## Focus, attention & the call-to-action

- **H1. If** a screen has multiple competing CTAs/links of similar visual weight **then** users split attention, click the first clickable thing, and the primary action leaks **because** attention is scarce and the action competes with everything else — **fix:** one clear primary CTA per screen, visually distinct in color/size; demote or remove the rest.

- **H2. If** a screen is visually overcrowded with many items competing equally **then** users feel negative arousal and avoidance and can't focus **because** clutter raises arousal in a negative direction and decision time rises with options (Hick's Law) — **fix:** establish clear visual hierarchy, cut item count, give primary content larger size/position.

- **H3. If** the primary CTA is below the fold, small, same-size as neighbors, or worded "Submit" **then** conversions drop **because** many users never scroll, can't find it, or don't know what it does — **fix:** put a prominent action-worded CTA ("Buy Now", "Register Here") above the fold, repeat it ~every screen (~400–500px) on long pages, and don't crowd it.

- **H4. If** a button/affordance doesn't look clickable or the page gist isn't graspable in a ~2-second glance **then** users don't know where to act **because** we scan, we don't read — **fix:** make buttons look like buttons; apply the two-second rule.

- **H5. If** a frequently-used primary control looks flat/recessed, or a rare/destructive control (delete, power) looks prominent and clickable **then** users mis-engage or trigger destructive actions **because** affordances suggest the wrong action — **fix:** make primary actions protrude/high-contrast; recess or de-emphasize rare/dangerous ones.

## Scent, continuity & congruency

- **H6. If** the ad/search keyword and message don't appear on the landing page in the first screen **then** users bounce **because** they judge ad-to-page relevance in <1 second and lose scent — **fix:** echo the search term in the headline, surface matching content first, keep look/feel uniform page to page.

- **H7. If** clicking an item leads to a page where that exact item is absent **then** continuity breaks **because** users expected to see what they clicked — **fix:** carry the clicked image/item onto the next page (one reported case saw a double-digit conversion lift).

- **H8. If** the value proposition overwhelms the page and buries the thing the visitor searched for, or competing messages collide **then** the page is incongruent and friction rises **because** every element must work toward ONE value prop and goal — **fix:** tie the value prop to the offered thing, lead with what the visitor came for, remove conflicting offers.

- **H9. If** the value proposition is a generic claim ("best service / lowest price") **then** it fails **because** online users instantly comparison-shop and overused claims read as noise — **fix:** make it unique, desirable, and provable.

- **H10. If** product/copy is manufacturer boilerplate shared by competitors ("me-too" syndrome) or the brand looks like every competitor (stock photos, boilerplate, generic palette) **then** it's forgettable and gives no reason to choose it **because** without cognitive contrast the brain has no pattern to latch onto — **fix:** write custom copy and craft a distinct, authentic brand personality and visual identity.

## Trust, professionalism & social signals

- **H11. If** a checkout / money / data-sensitive product has no security/trust signals or looks cheap, flat, or unpolished **then** users feel unprotected, distrust it, and abandon **because** entering sensitive info reactivates fears, and we assume scammers make bad-looking products; trust is a gut/aesthetic judgment — **fix:** add recognized, verifiable security seals and guarantees near sensitive fields AND invest in refined visual craft.

- **H12. If** trust badges/seals/testimonials are present **then** verify they're genuine, third-party, and FTC-compliant **because** self-issued/paid seals correlate with *lower* trustworthiness and fake testimonials violate FTC rules — **fix:** use verifiable certification and real, disclosed endorsements.

- **H13. If** trust/assurance elements are added without persona analysis **then** they can backfire and lower conversion **because** the same assurance reassures one audience and raises suspicion in another — **fix:** test assurances per-site/per-persona; never copy a competitor's tactic blindly.

- **H14. If** appeals are impersonal/machine-generated **then** users pattern-match them as spam and ignore **because** we have an automatic rejection response to impersonal sources — **fix:** be authentic and personal (faces, handwriting, names).

## Personality, tone & emotional fit

- **H15. If** the product expresses one personality in visuals but another in copy/voice/error messages **then** users distrust it **because** inconsistent personality signals read as dishonesty/instability — **fix:** unify tone across visuals, microcopy, voice, errors, packaging via a voice-and-tone guide.

- **H16. If** humor or a light tone is applied to serious/high-stakes content (finance, health, hardship) **then** it feels tone-deaf and erodes trust **because** personality must match content and audience — **fix:** shift to warmth, empathy, and human stories instead of jokes.

- **H17. If** personality/humor reads as a contrived gimmick **then** users sense it and lose trust **because** brains detect inauthenticity as "bad for me" — **fix:** base the persona on the real people behind the product; dial humor up to, not past, where it distracts from the message.

- **H18. If** a mascot/assistant/animation interrupts, blocks the workflow, or delivers task feedback/errors **then** it becomes hated **because** blocking a busy user is always bad — **fix:** keep delightful elements off the critical path; never let them deliver errors/stats/help.

- **H19. If** an error/system message blames or criticizes the user in harsh language **then** it creates a strong, lasting negative memory that distorts the whole impression **because** negatives are over-weighted (hedonic asymmetry) — **fix:** soften with empathy/ownership/reassurance; flatter novices.

## Interaction, feedback & social rules

- **H20. If** a button/link/action produces no immediate feedback (or an action >a few seconds shows no progress) **then** users feel ignored, lose trust, or can't course-correct **because** we read non-response as a status snub and feedback must be timely/clear/actionable — **fix:** give quick visual (+optional sound) feedback for every action; show a progress indicator for delays.

- **H21. If** the product takes a dominant role (goes first, commanding language, high arousal) when it should be an assistant **then** users feel they can't control it and get frustrated **because** for tool/assistant roles we want the product to defer (submissive) and let the user lead — **fix:** wait for user input, use deferential language, give the user power/status.

- **H22. If** content violates Quality, Quantity, Relevance, or Clarity (Grice's maxims) **then** users get frustrated/angry **because** product conversations are judged by human conversation rules — **fix:** make copy correct, appropriately sized, on-topic, and clearly relevant.

- **H23. If** the interface offers no greeting on arrival or acknowledgment on exit/loading **then** it feels rude **because** products are expected to follow human etiquette — **fix:** acknowledge arrival and signal transitions.

## Arousal, color & motion

- **H24. If** a task-oriented/high-stress interface (medical, finance, error-recovery) is aesthetically busy/arousing **then** it overwhelms already-stressed users and impairs problem-solving **because** stress narrows attention to tunnel vision — **fix:** keep it calm and less rich; put all relevant info close at hand with clear feedback.

- **H25. If** an experiential/entertainment product is visually flat, gray, low-contrast **then** it fails to attract/motivate **because** low arousal = low motivation when there's no task challenge — **fix:** add saturated color, larger imagery, motion, personality.

- **H26. If** a priority element has low saturation/contrast, or a large high-arousal hero dominates a learning/task screen **then** it either fails to draw attention or makes users remember only the image, not the content **because** saturation/contrast/size drive arousal and attention — **fix:** raise saturation/contrast of priority elements; reserve big arousing imagery for experiential contexts.

- **H27. If** hue carries meaning for a global audience with no cultural check **then** the intended read may invert **because** hue meaning is culturally conditioned (red = stop in the West, luck in China) though saturation→arousal is universal — **fix:** rely on saturation/contrast for arousal; localize/validate hue semantics.

- **H28. If** flashing/neon colors, banners, or competing animations dominate **then** they distract from the goal **because** attention is pulled from the conversion goal — **fix:** replace with a static image that supports the value prop.

## Forms, choices & cognitive load

- **H29. If** a form asks more than the minimum essential fields (fax, split name, city+state when zip is given) **then** completion drops sharply **because** long forms feel intrusive and raise privacy fears — **fix:** strip to essentials, autofill/auto-advance, never put a "Clear" button beside "Submit".

- **H30. If** a step offers too many options **then** users freeze, abandon, or regret **because** choice overload reduces both decision rate and satisfaction — **fix:** reduce/group/curate options and provide intelligent defaults.

- **H31. If** a control requires non-obvious logical leaps to understand **then** cognitive overhead taxes the user even when the action is physically easy **because** each "if I do this then maybe that" costs energy — **fix:** make the action and its consequence obvious; reserve required thinking for input that genuinely shapes the user's outcome.

- **H32. If** a required field has no sensible default and many users can't answer it **then** they fabricate answers or abandon — **fix:** default where non-response is acceptable; remove un-answerable, un-defaultable questions.

## Behavior-change mechanics

- **H33. If** the desired action is implied but never explicitly stated/asked **then** few act **because** every mental leap between cue and action loses people — **fix:** state the action directly and ask for it; specificity + personal phrasing lifts conversion.

- **H34. If** the design relies on the user remembering to act later with no cue **then** the behavior never starts **because** the cue is the first and most-skipped stage — **fix:** ask now (notification/email/big "Act now" button) or elicit implementation intentions tied to an existing trigger.

- **H35. If** a benefit is framed as a future gain while cost/effort is immediate **then** users procrastinate **because** of temporal myopia (we overvalue the present) — **fix:** frame benefits as immediate/near-term and push effort into the future.

- **H36. If** a flow breaks the action into more than ~12 small steps **then** it overwhelms **because** there's tension between manageable chunks and too many of them — **fix:** combine into the largest still-feasible chunks.

- **H37. If** the success metric is a "state of mind" (education, confidence, awareness) **then** the team argues endlessly and may build the wrong thing **because** states of mind are unmeasurable and usually not the real goal — **fix:** redefine success as an observable real-world outcome.

- **H38. If** the target action is several causal steps removed from the outcome **then** the leap is risky and likely fails **because** the further the product is from the action, the less it drives it — **fix:** target the behavior that directly causes the outcome; surface and test the assumed link.

- **H39. If** the product pays users to do something they already had intrinsic reason to do **then** the behavior collapses when payment stops **because** extrinsic incentives crowd out intrinsic motivation — **fix:** connect the action to the user's existing reason; avoid direct payments for repeated voluntary behavior.

- **H40. If** a peer comparison tells users they're already above the norm with no further approval **then** they relax and do less (boomerang effect) **because** people conform toward the norm — **fix:** when comparing to peers, pick a reference group doing better and add explicit social approval for above-norm performance.

## Journey, waiting & the unhappy path

- **H41. If** a wait/loading time is unannounced or under-estimated **then** users feel it as longer and frustration spikes **because** unmanaged uncertainty is the real pain, not duration — **fix:** show expected time up front, slightly over-quote, and entertain/inform during the wait.

- **H42. If** answering a question bounces the user between pages/agents, or the menu/flow offers no escape to a human/fitting option **then** navigation is failing and users abandon **because** the point-of-contact can't resolve on the spot — **fix:** equip the contact point to resolve; add filtration/clear common paths; always provide an off-ramp.

- **H43. If** a literal/correct answer ignores the user's real intent **then** the response fails though accurate **because** users ask surface questions for deeper goals — **fix:** answer the underlying need.

- **H44. If** a standardized flow has no path for atypical users **then** edge-case users (accessibility, locale, novices) hit dead ends **because** the process accommodates only the average — **fix:** add explicit, discoverable service-attention paths for non-standard cases.

- **H45. If** the design hasn't been red-teamed for the unhappy path / abuse cases **then** catastrophic failure modes stay hidden **because** survivorship bias hides what didn't survive — **fix:** run a red-team / speculative worst-case exercise before shipping.

- **H46. If** peripheral/in-between surfaces (loading, confirmations, empty states, 404s) are neglected **then** you miss cheap wins **because** low expectations there make small investment high-yield — **fix:** deliberately design the in-between spaces.

- **H47. If** the maker hasn't physically walked the flow end-to-end as the user **then** real friction stays invisible **because** they judge from inside their own assumptions — **fix:** do a full walkthrough from a novice/limited-ability vantage.

## Content fluency & ordering bias

- **H48. If** content is dense, wall-of-text, typographically bland **then** users judge the task as hard and bounce before reading **because** cognitive fluency: perceived reading-ease = perceived task-ease, and easy-to-process info is believed more — **fix:** break into scannable chunks, add imagery/headers; use plain language for safety/health/legal/critical info.

- **H49. If** a most-recent or arbitrary item sits at the top of a list **then** users read it as most authoritative/important **because** they conflate visual hierarchy with importance (serial position effect) — **fix:** order by genuine importance, or pair opposing views.

- **H50. If** an evaluative/judgment screen shows name, photo, school, or prior employer **then** decisions get biased **because** mere-exposure, name-pronunciation, and pattern-matching nonconsciously inflate ratings — **fix:** anonymize/defer biasing-but-irrelevant fields during initial review.

- **H51. If** a high-stakes or harm-prone action is maximally frictionless **then** users make fast, regret-prone errors, or post hurtful content impulsively **because** speed defeats deliberate thinking — **fix:** add purposeful friction — confirmation, slow content, a two-sentence reflective prompt.

## Stakeholder & process biases

- **H52. If** stakeholders won't accept solid research/design **then** there's a hidden incentive misaligned with the goal **because** people act on how they're compensated/measured, not logic — **fix:** find out how they're compensated; realign to a shared higher purpose.

- **H53. If** you pitch a change by emphasizing the upside/gains **then** risk-averse stakeholders dig in **because** talking up positives increases risk aversion and losses hurt roughly twice as much as equivalent gains please — **fix:** reframe as the concrete loss they incur by not changing.

- **H54. If** sign-offs are scheduled late in a session, before lunch, or mid-week **then** stakeholders default to conservative/safe choices **because** decision fatigue depletes judgment — **fix:** put the riskiest decision first, schedule after a meal, provide food.

- **H55. If** feedback is collected openly (hands raised, highest-paid speaks first) **then** you get false consensus **because** the bandwagon effect suppresses dissent — **fix:** collect anonymously (sticky notes, agree/disagree without reply); ensure a valid path for dissent.

## Ethics / dark-pattern alarms

- **H56. If** a consent/marketing/data/add-on checkbox is pre-checked (opt-out) **then** it's presumed consent the user didn't intend **because** inertia means most never uncheck it — **fix:** make it opt-in (unchecked) and state plainly what enabling it does.

- **H57. If** an optional field sits between known-required elements **then** users assume it's required and submit unwanted data **because** they pattern-match adjacency to requiredness — **fix:** separate optional fields and mark requiredness explicitly.

- **H58. If** cancelling/unsubscribing/deleting is harder than signing up **then** it's an asymmetric-effort trap **because** it relies on friction + loss aversion to retain unwilling users — **fix:** make exit as easy as entry; single-click unsubscribe.

- **H59. If** a strikethrough "original/was $X" price, countdown timer, or "X people viewing/low stock" appears **then** suspect fabricated anchors/scarcity **because** they inflate perceived value/urgency without basis **— fix:** show only true, verifiable reference prices and real availability; reserve scarcity for genuine one-offs.

- **H60. If** total price/fees/"savings" are shown without the breakdown, or hard terms are revealed only after the user invests effort/data **then** the math is hidden / it's a sunk-cost trap **because** users accept presented numbers and commitment makes them complete a deal they'd have declined — **fix:** show line-item math and all-in price, and disclose price + required info before the user invests.

- **H61. If** the easy/highlighted path leads to the company's preferred outcome and the user's interest needs extra clicks **then** desire lines are weaponized **because** people follow the path of least resistance — **fix:** put the user-beneficial choice on the default/easy path.

- **H62. If** a pricing layout includes an obvious inferior "decoy" or shows the second-best option first, or any layout visually favors one option **then** it's contrast/anchoring steering **because** we judge by comparison, never in a vacuum — **fix:** apply the disclosure test; present options on honest, comparable terms.

- **H63. If** the product uses in-app tokens/credits instead of real money **then** suspect price obfuscation and breakage **because** tokens detach price points from real cost and profit from unspent balances — **fix:** display real-currency equivalents and clear expiry/refund terms.

- **H64. If** rewards arrive on an unpredictable schedule (loot boxes, gacha, streaks, infinite pull-to-refresh) **then** it's a partial-reinforcement compulsion loop **because** variable rewards maximize time-on-task — **fix:** disclose odds, offer transparent/fixed rewards, add stopping cues.

- **H65. If** a progress bar starts pre-filled ("2 of 10 free") **then** it's the endowed-progress effect manufacturing commitment **because** artificial head-starts increase effort to finish — **fix:** ensure the goal and effort represent real value to the user.

- **H66. If** the only path to status/progress is buying it (pay-to-skip, energy timers) **then** the design throttles to monetize impatience **because** engineered friction is sold back as "convenience" — **fix:** don't manufacture frustration solely to sell its removal.

- **H67. If** a survey/CTA/question is leading ("why are so many switching to us?", "do you think this site has a pleasant personality?") **then** it's a push-poll implanting a conclusion **because** the question primes the answer — **fix:** use neutral, open-ended wording.

- **H68. If** "free" is offered (free shipping/trial/credit report) **then** check where the cost is recouped **because** "free" suspends rational evaluation and often hides downstream charges/auto-billing — **fix:** disclose what triggers charges, and when, before sign-up.

- **H69. If** legal/privacy/opt-out copy is long, dense, or placed away from the action it governs **then** it's deliberate obfuscation **because** the designer is betting users won't read or find it — **fix:** plain-language disclosure at the point of decision.

- **H70. If** change is rolled out as many tiny steps rather than one announced change **then** suspect a "slippery slope" to evade backlash **because** individually inoffensive changes avoid revolt — **fix:** disclose cumulative direction and material changes prominently.

- **H71. If** social features rely on anonymity **then** expect disinhibition / antinormative behavior **because** deindividuation removes accountability — **fix:** set situational norms, persistent pseudonyms, visible moderation.

## Change management & polish

- **H72. If** a redesign is dumped on users all at once with no foreshadowing (even if strictly improved) **then** you risk backlash **because** unfamiliarity breeds discomfort and forced change breeds resentment — **fix:** foreshadow (teaser), roll out gradually (velvet-rope), offer an opt-back-out link ("you may", not "you must").

- **H73. If** an outage/error occurs and you go silent or lead with jokes **then** stressed users churn **because** facts and reassurance must precede levity — **fix:** explain swiftly, honestly, update regularly, *then* optionally redirect with a goodwill gesture.

- **H74. If** a "minor" detail (packaging, empty/error states, confirmations, microcopy, loading) is left unconsidered **then** it silently leaks neutral or negative emotion **because** every touchpoint emits emotion whether designed or not — **fix:** treat each detail as an intentional emotion touchpoint.

- **H75. If** any out-of-place element exists (typo, dead link, broken image, mismatched theme, stale data) **then** it can negate the entire moment's credibility **because** the themed experience is fragile and one contradiction breaks the illusion — **fix:** treat detail defects as experience-breaking, not cosmetic.

- **H76. If** the product is loaded with features to look "powerful" but is hard to use **then** initial satisfaction turns to frustration **because** perceived complexity reads as power but usability sustains the relationship — **fix:** strike a dynamic balance — powerful enough to seem capable, simple enough that the user stays in control.

- **H77. If** an assistive/accessibility feature visibly marks the user as needing help, or a product for women is just "shrunk and pinked", or identity is a fixed radio set **then** it stigmatizes/erases and gets rejected **because** users won't sacrifice dignity/self-image and notational bias privileges the first option — **fix:** accommodate invisibly; balance real needs; use open fields, never "Other".

- **H78. If** photos of people are cropped wide/full-body when emotional connection is the goal **then** viewers focus on appearance over personality and feel less empathy **because** the face-ism ratio: tight face crops trigger emotional response — **fix:** crop tightly to the face for cause/empathy content.

- **H79. If** a redesign relies on testing thousands of multivariate combinations at once, or makes drastic changes with no prior behavioral knowledge **then** learning is diluted and tests fail **because** brute force can't isolate cause — **fix:** change one thing at a time, hypothesis-driven; small iterative wins compound.

- **H80. If** an audience is apathetic toward the experience **then** content is likely irrelevant or poorly presented **because** great delivery only rescues genuinely good content — **fix:** fix content relevance/quality first, then add engaging delivery; verify with a small batch of usability tests.
