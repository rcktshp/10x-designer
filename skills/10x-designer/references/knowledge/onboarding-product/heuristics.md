# Onboarding & Product — Critique Heuristics

"If X then problem Y, fix Z." Deduplicated across the cluster.

## Up-front instruction & tours

- **If** onboarding is a front-loaded video, slideshow, carousel, tour, or coachmarks shown before the user can act, or that just narrate the UI **then** it's skipped, dismissed, forgotten, and can make the product feel *harder* **because** passive instruction is out of context and signals a confusing UI tacked-on onboarding (NN/g: tutorial users rated taught concepts *more* difficult) **— fix:** weave guidance into the interaction; fix the underlying UI; if a tour stays, make it guide through DOING an action, not restate button labels.
- **If** onboarding is mostly tooltips pointing at buttons/menus **then** the interface isn't self-explanatory and the onboarding is product-centric ("look what we built") **because** a good label shouldn't need a tooltip repeating the word **— fix:** fix UI clarity; reserve guidance for genuinely novel concepts.
- **If** the post-signup flow shows expository "limbo"/intro screens or long loading/pending states **then** users fast-forward, feel stranded, and may leave **because** earlier touchpoints should already have introduced the product and limbo time is unmanaged **— fix:** keep limbo only to capture rarely-changing setup info; show the real app faintly behind any focus task ("Emerald City"); fill waits with progress or useful interim content.
- **If** users are shown all advanced features/options at once **then** they stall at the first hurdle **because** of information overload **— fix:** reveal features progressively as usage evidence accumulates (shortcuts after heavy use; export once tracking lots of data).

## Signup, friction & detours

- **If** a signup/account/EULA wall blocks all use up front (or requires code install / data import / credit card before anything) **then** expect abandonment, fake accounts, and a damaged first impression — non-technical users and team members get blocked **because** people haven't committed yet and security friction becomes the first impression **— fix:** defer signup/technical steps; offer a free sample / guest mode; let anyone create an account and explore (Intercom email→signup rose ~30%→45% after a CSV importer deferred JS install).
- **If** the signup form asks for anything not strictly needed now (phone, industry, credit card, CAPTCHA, long form) **then** each field turns away high-potential users (account fatigue; 92% have abandoned rather than recover a login — Janrain) **because** "the database requires it" is not a user benefit **— fix:** cut every non-essential field; use progressive profiling and social login (Facebook consumer / Google B2B); if many fields are truly required, split into smaller steps.
- **If** signup forces email confirmation before app access, or an action requires leaving the flow (open inbox, search a coupon) to continue **then** users drift off-path and may never return **because** the detour is a Point of Disconnect that breaks momentum **— fix:** grant provisional access + a non-blocking "confirm your email" banner; allow continuing with limited functionality; verify after the action.
- **If** onboarding presents a big multi-field form up front **then** new users are overwhelmed and drop off **because** attention is scarce and value is deferred **— fix:** ask one piece of info at a time with microcopy; lead through *required* actions only; reach the aha fast.
- **If** a button/form submit gives no immediate feedback, or the UI is slow/janky/buggy **then** the user turns inward ("did I break it?") and new users abandon disproportionately **because** silence breaks the conversational "I hear you" and poor performance breaks trust before onboarding matters (FT page-load experiment) **— fix:** make every interaction responsive and human; ensure fast first-run load with no layout jank.

## Steps, scope & "what next?"

- **If** a workflow has steps with no bearing on the immediate goal (confirm email, name the survey, manual save, click-through that adds no insight/decision, export→re-save→email loops) **then** each is a needless drop-off point **because** steps prevent achievement, not just delay it **— fix:** cut, defer to after the win, automate, or default them (survey: 8 → 3).
- **If** a setup/first-run flow keeps accumulating "just one more thing" (legal, permissions, promos, feature setup) **then** it overwhelms and makes the product seem complex **because** short-term memory is limited and unscoped flows have gravitational pull **— fix:** map the journey, scope actions, distribute them over time.
- **If** a design starts as a screen full of UI with no defined user/intent/flow, or a first interaction exists but its later steps (confirmation, notification, admin handling, resolution) are undesigned **then** it's designed backward and half-built with dead ends **because** the screen is one moment, not the experience, and the user's intent is never satisfied **— fix:** define User → Intent → Success → action chain; keep asking "what happens next?" until intent is met.
- **If** a mockup shows data/images/categories/UGC with no way to create/manage it **then** complexity is underestimated and the build is far larger than it looks **because** every output needs an input, rules, and zero/error states **— fix:** circle every output; define who changes it, how, with what restrictions and empty/error states.
- **If** a feature ballooned into a multi-month project, or keeps expanding to cover the user's whole working day **then** it's likely to be cut, demoralize the team, and bloat toward a tiny audience **because** large scope invites change and rigid JTBD leads to solving everything **— fix:** find the core, ship small; stop at the step where leaders exist, end-users change, or you add no value.
- **If** core functionality is incomplete but the team is adding new features **then** expansion sits on a weak foundation **because** SaaS rewards reliable+complete over bloated+buggy **— fix:** fill the gaps in the core job before broadening scope.

## The first win & value framing

- **If** the chosen first win depends on others, on IT/clearance, on technical steps (embed code, tracking snippet), or on content the user doesn't have yet, or banks on an intent the user doesn't arrive with (Twitter "tweet now"; Timely "schedule a tweet") **then** a large share of signups stall before value and activation craters **because** the win isn't a safe bet on a current intent **— fix:** pick a self-contained, ready-now win on an intent everyone has (Twitter → "follow"; Timely → suggested quotes lifted activation 7%→70%).
- **If** the value/"aha" is only revealed after long setup or a high-touch demo **then** users quit before payoff and most never reach it **because** the reward isn't frontloaded **— fix:** surface a relevant life-improvement up front, contextualized; generate the aha before signup where possible.
- **If** pre-signup copy describes features/attributes ("patented green stalk") rather than the user's improved self, or makes a high-level claim that never answers "ok, how?" **then** the claim reads as dubious and users must assemble meaning themselves **because** people want to know how the product improves THEIR life **— fix:** lead with one clear improved-user outcome, then follow with the concrete mechanism (Buffer's 24-word value prop).
- **If** the home/landing page is sparse "just sign up" like Twitter/Facebook **then** most visitors who "have no clue" leave **because** only true household names can presume recognition **— fix:** be comprehensive about value; don't copy companies much larger than you.
- **If** marketing's promise doesn't match the landing/signup it points to **then** users arrive misaligned and are doomed from the start **because** no wizard fixes a wrong expectation **— fix:** check every touchpoint for consistency; maintain the scent of information; carry one story/tone/promise across all touchpoints.
- **If** the team can't name six believable ways the product improves a user's life **then** the value is suspect **because** value, not features, drives retention **— fix:** sketch "what can the user do now that they couldn't before?"; rethink usefulness.

## Jobs, personas & competition

- **If** a product/feature is justified by user attributes (age, role, demographics) or written as "As a [persona] I can [feature] so that [benefit]" **then** the rationale is weak and failures are undiagnosable **because** attributes don't explain motivation and implementation is coupled to the assumed benefit **— fix:** rewrite as a solution-free Job Story (When/I want to/so I can).
- **If** a UI element can't be traced to a job or anxiety it resolves **then** it's probably noise/decoration **because** every element should earn its place by advancing the job **— fix:** annotate each element with its job; remove or redesign those that resolve none.
- **If** the product surfaces category-internal detail (precipitation depth, air pressure) instead of answering the user's real question ("will it rain?") **then** it optimizes the wrong thing **because** users buy the job outcome, not domain intricacies **— fix:** design to the question the user actually asks (Intercom: a "worse" auto-redacting shareable map did a better job).
- **If** a competitive analysis only lists same-category products **then** real competitors are missed **because** disruption comes from oblique angles (milkshakes vs. bananas; newspapers vs. games) **— fix:** list everything hired for the same job, including non-software and "do nothing."
- **If** a new product offers only incremental improvement over the incumbent **then** it won't get mainstream adoption **because** of the 9X Effect (3x × 3x perception gap) **— fix:** aim for ~10x improvement, or attack a non-quality force (anxiety/habit).
- **If** a comparison/landing page only argues "we're better" **then** inertia still blocks adoption **because** quality is one of four forces **— fix:** also reduce anxiety (show switching is quick/safe) and dissolve status-quo attachment (Apple "I'm a Mac" worked all four forces).
- **If** users sign up, understand the value, yet don't adopt **then** an indirect-competing desire may conflict (loved A/B testing but loved clean code more) **because** a second desire is incompatible with using you **— fix:** reposition so the outcome isn't in conflict, or message down the competing desire.

## Empty states, completion & emotion

- **If** a new user lands on a blank/empty state ("Your campaign is empty," "You don't have any friends") **then** they lack a model of success, feel blamed, and bounce **because** you'd never say that to a customer in person **— fix:** seed a pre-made example with best-practice tips and a clear CTA (Trello welcome board, Basecamp "Explore Basecamp!"); prefer content-as-tutorial over fake dummy data.
- **If** a completed setup/quest simply disappears with no acknowledgement **then** you waste the dopamine peak and Peak-End opportunity and momentum stalls **because** the action isn't reciprocated **— fix:** congratulate them with a clear success state and ride into a next suggestion.
- **If** there's no visible checklist/progress meter for early key activities **then** users don't know what success looks like **because** key activities aren't called out **— fix:** add explicit checklists/meters with endowed progress (pre-check some items, Quora-style) for the most valuable early actions.
- **If** a next-steps checklist is a team wish-list including items some users don't need, or completion is a gamified progress/profile-strength meter forcing business tasks ("invite 5 friends" before "complete") **then** the whole list loses meaning and you optimize what the business wants **because** irrelevant items poison the list and 100% ≠ solving the user's problem **— fix:** reserve checklists for related steps most users complete; base progress on the user's declared goal; show the 3 most effective next steps.
- **If** faces/testimonials are clustered together, or social proof leans on vanity numbers ("200k followers") **then** attention scatters and persuasion drops **because** prospects care about results they can attain **— fix:** spread faces across page and across pages (including in-app); show concrete outcomes ("$984,641,452 earned to date").
- **If** interpersonal motivators (faces, social proof, human help, encouragement) vanish after signup **then** in-app engagement suffers **because** they drive engagement just as well inside the product **— fix:** reuse them inside the app, not only in marketing.

## Presentation, patterns & consistency

- **If** overlays/popups interrupt users mid-task, or inline cues are styled like ads / used too often **then** they're ignored even when important and trigger banner blindness **because** disruption reads as in-the-way (BYU/Google security-dialog study) **— fix:** show overlays only at natural pauses or reactively; limit and avoid collisions; style inline cues to complement content.
- **If** a wizard is used for a frequent/simple action or its screens just serve information, or a repeatable action always shows first-time guidance **then** it feels tedious like a slideshow in disguise and annoys returning users **because** wizards suit only one-time, complex, action-oriented setup and scaffolding wasn't removed **— fix:** use wizards only for one-time setup; remove scaffolding on later uses or make guidance opt-in (Basecamp "Want help?").
- **If** screens are inconsistent (buttons/icons/fonts/headers/voice vary across marketing, app, emails, errors) **then** cognitive load rises and the experience feels incoherent **because** users expect consistency to orient themselves **— fix:** maintain a working style guide and one consistent voice; enforce in every review.
- **If** a popular pattern (infinite feed, swipe, grid) is adopted without asking what behavior it serves **then** it may be a usability nightmare for your use case **because** patterns are popular for *their* task, not yours; consumer patterns often fail in B2B **— fix:** ask "what behavior am I trying to achieve, and what pattern best supports it?"
- **If** the first run fails accessibility, localization, or offers only binary/forced choices **then** entire audiences abandon and revenue is lost **because** they aren't met where they are (Deque: ~70% of sites had critical blockers for visually-impaired users) **— fix:** support assistive tech/device settings; detect/offer language & region; provide non-binary options.
- **If** a checkout/critical flow keeps full header/nav and distractions **then** users wander off mid-task and abandon **because** intent-to-complete leaks away **— fix:** strip navigation during checkout (Amazon); re-engage with cross-sells only after the task completes.

## Dark patterns & ethics

- **If** a default opt-in is pre-checked, a subscription is buried in fine print, cancellation is deliberately hard, or free-sample work is held hostage to force signup **then** it's a dark pattern **because** it benefits the company against a fully-informed user's wishes (JustFab's hidden ~$39.95/mo VIP) **— fix:** make exchanges of value explicit and consent active; never hold work hostage.

## Measurement, research & ownership

- **If** onboarding success is judged by signups, installs, tutorial completion, or any vanity metric / surface number (clicks on Save) alone **then** the team optimizes the wrong thing and may hurt retention **because** these don't indicate stickiness or tie to a business goal **— fix:** pair acquisition with retention/engagement; measure the intended *behavior change*.
- **If** an A/B test ships with no pre-defined success threshold or health metrics **then** results are uninterpretable and may quietly harm retention/revenue **because** you can't tell win from noise and a single metric is gameable **— fix:** write a falsifiable hypothesis (belief + quantifiable pass condition) and list danger metrics before launch.
- **If** onboarding/feedback triggers fire on calendar time (day 1, 2, 7) and blast everyone identically **then** you over-message some, under-serve others, train users to ignore you, and address the trial-conversion decision too late **because** elapsed time says nothing about value reached and the decision is made early **— fix:** trigger by behavior/usage milestones, segment, and drive core value in the first ~3 days with day-3/day-7 success actions.
- **If** research asks users "will you buy this / how much would you pay / what should we build?", or the interviewer leads/sells/helps the struggling user **then** answers are unreliable/biased **because** people predict the future poorly and you hear what you want **— fix:** ask about past behavior, observe real usage, "show me," let them struggle, listen far more than you talk.
- **If** you don't talk to recently-switched users **then** "insights" are guesses from analytics/NPS **because** emotional motivations don't appear in dashboards **— fix:** interview users right after the switching moment, anchored on real events, traced back before they found you.
- **If** the team only measures the deepest "why," or hasn't run its own onboarding recently **then** valuable mid-layer insight is lost and it loses touch with the experience/seams **because** the gold is in Useful/Usable/Desirable layers and people onboard once then forget **— fix:** explore each why-layer fully; periodically re-run the full flow with fresh eyes.
- **If** transitions between flow stages feel jarring/restyled/contextually wrong (post-install UI references products you didn't buy) **then** the onboarding mirrors the org chart ("reads like an office directory") **because** teams handed off without shared ownership (Conway's Law) **— fix:** assign one onboarding owner per team, align on a shared goal, enforce deliberate domain overlap at transitions, review end-to-end.
- **If** a new feature ships with only a one-off "announcement" email to all users **then** it's ignored and becomes non-monetizable bloat **because** "get it launched" is the wrong goal **— fix:** announce in-context at the moment of intent; re-onboard existing users when they're in the relevant area (Microsoft: ~90% of requested "new" features already existed).
- **If** onboarding ends at signup and there's no mechanism to flag declining usage or form social bonds / lifecycle emails **then** engaged-customer value is lost, churn rises, and win-back comes too late **because** users haven't become experts or dropped competitors **— fix:** treat onboarding as continuous; build an early-warning system; use social accountability and lifecycle emails (welcome / progress / come-back / running-out-of-time) as connective tissue.
- **If** UX/design is brought in late "to review" an existing product **then** real problems (wrong users, no demand, wrong flow) can't be fixed by surface tweaks **because** UX is the whole experience, not polish **— fix:** involve design immediately, if not sooner.
