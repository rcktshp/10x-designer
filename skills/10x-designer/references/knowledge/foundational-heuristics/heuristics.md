# Foundational Heuristics — Named Frameworks & Laws (canonical reference)

The canonical, **cite-by-name** heuristic sets and cognitive laws. Kosmo should reference these explicitly in critiques (e.g. "fails Nielsen #1 Visibility of system status", "violates Fitts's Law", "per Cialdini, social proof…"). Overlaps with other clusters by design — this is the named-canon index.

## Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics (NN/g)
1. **Visibility of system status** — keep users informed via timely feedback.
2. **Match between system and the real world** — speak users' language; familiar concepts, not jargon.
3. **User control and freedom** — clearly marked exits; easy undo/redo.
4. **Consistency and standards** — same word/action means the same thing (follow platform conventions).
5. **Error prevention** — eliminate error-prone conditions or confirm before commit.
6. **Recognition rather than recall** — make options/info visible; don't force memory across screens.
7. **Flexibility and efficiency of use** — accelerators for experts; personalization.
8. **Aesthetic and minimalist design** — no irrelevant info competing with the essential.
9. **Help users recognize, diagnose, recover from errors** — plain language, name the problem, suggest a fix.
10. **Help and documentation** — searchable, task-focused, in context.

## Shneiderman's 8 Golden Rules of Interface Design
1. **Strive for consistency** — consistent sequences, terminology in prompts/menus/help.
2. **Seek universal usability** — accommodate novices→experts, disabilities, devices.
3. **Offer informative feedback** — feedback for every action, proportional to its significance.
4. **Design dialogs to yield closure** — group actions with a beginning, middle, end.
5. **Prevent errors** — design so serious errors are impossible; offer constructive recovery.
6. **Permit easy reversal of actions** — reversible actions reduce anxiety, encourage exploration.
7. **Keep users in control** — users are the initiators, not responders.
8. **Reduce short-term memory load** — don't make users remember info across displays.

## Tognazzini's First Principles of Interaction Design (selected of 19)
Aesthetics · **Anticipation** (bring info/tools to the user) · Autonomy · **Color** (never the sole cue) · Consistency · **Defaults** (smart, overridable) · **Discoverability** (visible controls) · **Efficiency of the user** (human time > machine time) · **Explorable interfaces** (undo; reversible) · **Fitts's Law** · Human-interface objects · **Latency reduction** (ack <50ms; mask delays) · Learnability · Metaphors (don't let them constrain) · **Protect users' work** (never lose data) · Readability · **Simplicity** (reduce, don't hide) · **State** (track location/history/prefs) · **Visible interfaces** (don't force mental maps).

## Gerhardt-Powals' 10 Cognitive Engineering Principles
Automate unwanted workload · Reduce uncertainty · Fuse data (low-level → summaries) · Present new info with meaningful aids to interpretation · Use names conceptually related to function · Group data in consistently meaningful ways · Limit data-driven tasks (use color/graphics) · Show only info needed at a given time · Provide multiple coding of data · Practice judicious redundancy.

## Cognitive laws (Laws of UX)
- **Hick's Law** — *decision time increases with the number and complexity of choices.* → limit options; break complex tasks into steps.
- **Fitts's Law** — *time to acquire a target is a function of distance to and size of the target.* → big targets, close to the user's focus (≥44px).
- **Miller's Law** — *the average person holds ~7 (±2) items in working memory.* → chunk content into meaningful groups.
- **Jakob's Law** — *users spend most of their time on other sites and prefer yours to work the same way.* → use familiar patterns; novelty must earn its cost.
- **Peak-End Rule** — *people judge an experience by its peak and its end, not the average.* → craft a memorable high point and a strong ending.
- **Doherty Threshold** — *productivity soars when system response <400ms.* → keep responses fast or mask with progress/skeletons. *(also in core-principles)*

## Fogg Behavior Model (FBM): **B = MAT**
A behavior occurs only when **Motivation + Ability + Trigger** converge at the same moment.
- **Motivation** — core motivators: pleasure/pain · hope/fear · social acceptance/rejection.
- **Ability (simplicity)** — make the behavior easier; simplicity = the user's **scarcest resource** at the moment of the trigger, across: time · money · physical effort · brain cycles · social deviance · non-routine.
- **Triggers** — must be noticed, associated with the behavior, and well-timed: **spark** (when motivation is low) · **facilitator** (when ability is low) · **signal** (when both are high — just remind).
- Design takeaway: when a behavior isn't happening, diagnose which factor is missing; **increasing ability (simplicity) usually beats piling on motivation.**

## Cialdini's 6 Principles of Influence (use ethically — see behavior-emotion-persuasion)
Reciprocation · Consistency/commitment · Social proof · Liking · Authority · Scarcity.

## Gestalt Principles of Grouping (perception → layout)
Proximity · Similarity · Closure · Good continuation · Common fate · Good form (Prägnanz). → grouping/spacing signals relatedness; use them to structure layouts and reduce perceived complexity.

## 10 Content Design Heuristics (UX content)
The Nielsen 10 re-expressed for words/copy: status (tell users what's happening) · real-world language (no jargon) · control/freedom (clear exits, undo) · consistency (style-guide labels) · error prevention (explain destructive actions) · recognition>recall (don't make them remember prior steps) · flexibility (multiple paths) · minimalist (cut extraneous copy) · recover from errors (plain, actionable) · help/docs (timely helper text, in-app guidance). *(see content-ux-writing for the full content checklist)*
