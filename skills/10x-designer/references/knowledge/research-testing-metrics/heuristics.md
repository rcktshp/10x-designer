# Research, Testing & Metrics — Critique Heuristics

Deduplicated "if X then problem Y, fix Z" rules.

---

## Findability, attention & visual hierarchy

- **If** users can't quickly answer "what is this, who runs it, what can I do here" on an entry/landing page **then** the entry point fails its orienting job ("the lost get loster") **because** users form working assumptions in the first ~50 ms and wrong ones cascade — **fix:** add a clear brief identity statement up top; cut clutter so the big picture reads in a few seconds.
- **If** a key element relies on subtle cues (hairline rules, low-contrast type, small size) or few/no participants ever fixate it **then** it's a findability/perception failure **because** users move fast at low resolution and miss subtlety — **fix:** boost salience/contrast/size/position; place the target where first fixations land; "make it loud enough to spot from 50 feet."
- **If** most users look AT a target but don't select it (long time-from-first-fixation-to-click) **then** it's a recognizability/comprehension failure **because** they don't understand it's the right target or it doesn't look clickable — **fix:** improve the label to plain literal language; strengthen the affordance; differentiate from semantic look-alikes.
- **If** an attention-grabbing element is looked at by many but clicked by few (Cox piggy bank: 70% looked, 23% clicked), or sits in a banner/large-graphic position **then** it's perceived as an ad / non-clickable **because** of banner blindness — **fix:** redesign so it doesn't resemble advertising; add an explicit verb CTA.
- **If** the scanpath to a target is long with frequent backtracking, or gaze oscillates between two elements before selecting **then** location is unexpected or a semantic competitor is causing comparison **because** two elements seem to serve the same function — **fix:** relocate to the expected position; clarify/differentiate labels; consolidate redundant navigation.
- **If** the page is an overcrowded "kitchen sink" with no focus **then** noise hides the signal **because** stakeholders keep adding things — **fix:** ruthlessly remove; consider "take something away" before adding; re-test the home page every round.
## Labels, mental model & IA

- **If** interface vocabulary differs from the words real users used (or from their search terms) **then** labels won't match the mental model **because** internal jargon isn't the user's language — **fix:** adopt participants' actual terminology in the UI; revise nav/copy to match search-query vocabulary.
- **If** a navigation label/action doesn't match what users expect it to do, or two labels overlap in meaning ("Book Travel" vs. "Reservations") **then** users dither/derail at the decision point **because** wording was written from the system's perspective — **fix:** one label per intent from interview vocabulary; apply the Hallway Test to each top nav item.
- **If** the flow/sequence doesn't reflect how users think about their workflow **then** task-flow mismatch — a top, high-leverage source of error **because** the system imposes its sequence — **fix:** restructure flow to the user's mental task sequence (a global fix that simplifies secondary problems).
- **If** top-level IA mirrors the org chart and the site keeps getting re-architected every ~2 years **then** the structure was never matched to the user's stable mental model **because** internally-derived IA needs constant correction — **fix:** derive IA once from mental spaces; spend energy on business goals instead.
- **If** the design relies on novelty for core navigation/interaction **then** it likely violates the mental model **because** there are no hooks into existing experience — **fix:** anchor to familiar conventions; reserve novelty for non-critical moments.
- **If** the design uses domain jargon assumed universally known **then** even "expert" users may not recognize it (the real-estate agent who asked "What's this?") **because** people don't know what you think they know — **fix:** verify terms with real users; don't assume shared vocabulary.
- **If** automated card-sort groups are taken at face value ("users made 20 groups → 20 nav items") **then** the IA bloats **because** interpretation needs UX judgment — **fix:** collapse user clusters into compact higher-order categories; verify with a second reviewer.
## Feature scope, value & coverage

- **If** a feature/content item maps to no behavior (tower) in the mental model **then** it likely serves no real user intent (the "stock ticker on the intranet") **because** it was added for internal/political reasons — **fix:** remove it, or verify with usage data + targeted research.
- **If** a feature has several equally-valid primary homes **then** it's under-defined or compound **because** a sharply-scoped feature serves one dominant intent — **fix:** redefine/split until it has one clear primary tower.
- **If** the design solves the prominent technically-interesting "5%" tasks well but the everyday top tasks poorly **then** effort is mis-prioritized **because** teams favor leading-edge features end users rarely touch — **fix:** ensure the ~75–80% everyday tasks work flawlessly before refining esoterica.
- **If** a product serves multiple audiences with little behavioral overlap in one monolith **then** every audience finds it hard to do anything **because** other audiences' features distract — **fix:** split into per-audience solutions (the "campus/buildings" model) with a central hub.
- **If** a product reissues "new & improved" tweaks, or copies competitor feature-sets out of fear **then** strategy is chasing repurchase/the competitive valley, not users **because** it solves the transaction not the enduring purpose — **fix:** pursue durable directions grounded in real people's differing purposes.
## Errors, recovery & critical tasks

- **If** the system shows "Unknown Error"/a bare code with no instruction, or has no clear undo/exit **then** error prevention & recovery is neglected **because** users can't escape what they don't understand — **fix:** plain-language messages saying what happened and what to do next; provide undo/redo and obvious exits.
- **If** error/empty/help copy says "Having trouble?", "Need more help?", "Are you sure?" **then** it implies user fault and provokes shame **because** the wording blames the person — **fix:** take the blame ("Password reset not working?", "How can we support you?").
- **If** a critical task can cause data loss, a support call, product damage, or harm (defibrillator, voting, two identical lights where one signals danger) **then** it's a maximum-severity / launch-blocker **because** even a 1% misread can injure (100% performance costs ~50× the cost of 95%) — **fix:** strict binary success tied to time + zero errors; differentiate by label/color/position; make recoverable.
- **If** a critical-product task is scored on partial credit **then** the metric is wrong **because** only complete, error-free, in-time success matters — **fix:** use strict binary success against a target goal.
## Tasks, prompts & moderation quality

- **If** a test task uses the exact unique wording shown on screen **then** the task is invalid **because** it becomes a word-matching exercise — **fix:** rewrite in the user's plain goal language without on-screen terms.
- **If** questions are leading/closed (begin "Did/Have/Are/Will…", trail off with suggested answers, or use the team's jargon) **then** participants nod along to your structure **because** people answer what they think you want — **fix:** ask the shortest possible open question, then stay silent; signal "lane changes" with transitions.
- **If** the facilitator helps, hints, answers questions, paraphrases, or over-uses "mm-hmm" **then** findings are compromised **because** influence biases behavior and active-listening cues signal "stop talking" — **fix:** stay neutral, reflect questions back ("What do you think?"), keep a poker face, tolerate silence.
- **If** the interviewer/stakeholder corrects the participant's terminology or pronunciation (the "Tye-vo" lesson) **then** rapport collapses and the power dynamic flips **because** the participant is publicly diminished in their own domain — **fix:** mirror their language; go meta if you must introduce a term.
- **If** the moderator reads the guide verbatim like a script **then** valuable emerging behaviors are missed **because** real sessions branch — **fix:** treat it as a flexible guide; chase relevant unexpected behavior; circle back to predetermined tasks at the end.
- **If** observers can be heard/seen by the participant, or ≥3 interviewers are in the room **then** the session is biased/awkward **because** the observer barrier broke and others shift the power dynamic — **fix:** insulate observers (muted line, hidden chat, moderator as filter); field a team of two.
- **If** a concept is presented as "here's something I've been working on" **then** participants soften criticism **because** you've activated an instinct to protect your feelings — **fix:** present neutrally ("early ideas I was asked to show you"); rehearse the reveal.
## Sampling, screening & study validity

- **If** screening criteria are vague ("active" user, "sharing") or attitude-based **then** you get wrong-fit participants **because** terms are interpreted differently — **fix:** specify behavior over attitude; confirm device/context presence; treat recruiting difficulty as data.
- **If** a recruiting screener reads like an ad, has >10 questions/grids, or lacks an open-ended question **then** qualified users dismiss/abandon it and fakers slip through **because** closed options telegraph the desired profile — **fix:** clean non-promotional copy, ≤10 eligibility-only questions, lead with "Why are you here today?", add trap questions.
- **If** recruiters cherry-pick "good-sounding" respondents or the incentive is set very high **then** the sample is biased by likability or flooded with money-seekers **because** choosing for likability isn't choosing for criteria — **fix:** take the most recent qualified recruit; raise incentives incrementally; scrutinize email/area-code clustering.
- **If** a product was validated only with internal staff who built/sell/support it **then** results are unreliable **because** they know too much — **fix:** recruit outsiders (or staff from unrelated departments).
- **If** results rely on a survey/focus group to answer behavioral questions ("Where did you have trouble?", "Is it user-friendly?") **then** the answer is worthless **because** stated want ≠ behavior — **fix:** replace with task elicitation; use opinions only to illuminate behavior. (Focus groups: "research theater.")
- **If** a "5 users find 85%" claim is made without stating p **then** it's likely false **because** 85%-at-5 only holds at p≈0.31; websites run p≈0.04 (5 users → ~14%) — **fix:** estimate p for your context; set n = ln(1−goal)/ln(1−p).
- **If** a single UX pro claims "I found all the major issues" after 5–10 users **then** be skeptical **because** the evaluator effect means evaluators find largely different sets (CUE: 60–75% by one team) — **fix:** add a second evaluator/team; maximize task coverage.
## Statistics & metric interpretation

- **If** a completion/conversion rate is reported as a bare percentage with no CI **then** the reader over-trusts it **because** a 5-user rate is typically ±21 points — **fix:** report the adjusted-Wald 95% CI (add 2 successes + 2 failures); never use raw Wald at n<100.
- **If** average task time is the arithmetic mean **then** it's inflated **because** task times are positively skewed — **fix:** report the geometric mean (n<25) or the median + CI (n>25); filter impossibly-fast or idle-long contaminated times.
- **If** two designs are compared on means/rates alone ("A scored higher") **then** the difference may be sampling noise **because** chance affects every sample — **fix:** run the appropriate significance test AND report the CI around the difference (effect size).
- **If** a decision rests on averages only ("82% success, 67s avg → goals met") **then** it may hide failure **because** combined per-participant goals can be met by far fewer people (only 38% in the book's example) — **fix:** score each participant pass/fail against the combined goal; report the distribution.
- **If** ordinal data (rankings, "excellent/good/fair", 4-level success) is averaged, or a scale uses −3…+3 / zero anchors **then** the statistic is invalid / responses skew **because** intervals between ranks aren't equal and people avoid zero/negatives — **fix:** report frequencies per level; use positive 5–7-point end-anchored scales.
- **If** a multipoint rating was collapsed to a top-box/binary (NPS promoters, "% 9–10") **then** sensitivity is lost **because** collapsing discards information — **fix:** analyze full-scale means; expect to need 2–4× the sample if you must collapse.
- **If** a SUS/questionnaire score is judged against the scale midpoint **then** average products are mislabeled **because** the SUS mean is 68, not 50 (PSSUQ ~2.82, not 4) — **fix:** interpret against empirical norms/percentiles; verify even-item reverse-scoring (~10% of datasets are miscoded).
- **If** many significance tests run on one dataset at α=0.05 **then** false positives balloon (20 tests ≈ 64% chance of ≥1) **because** of alpha inflation — **fix:** plan comparisons in advance, apply a correction, or flag exploratory findings as needing replication.
- **If** a one-tailed test is chosen after seeing data (or switched when results go the "wrong" way) **then** the real α inflates (a "1.5-tailed test") **because** you can't wish away a rejection region post hoc — **fix:** fix test direction before collecting; default two-tailed.
## A/B testing & experiment design

- **If** an A/B test pits two completely different page designs **then** a win is uninterpretable **because** many confounded variables changed — **fix:** test small isolated changes (button wording, photo placement), randomize the split (not morning vs. afternoon), triangulate with qual to isolate the driver.
- **If** test/control groups are formed by arrival order/"next N users", or the test runs only weekdays / a partial cycle **then** the sample is arbitrary and carries seasonal bias **because** usage differs by time/day/season — **fix:** draw one sample then randomize; run whole multiples of the business cycle (e.g., multiples of 7 days).
- **If** results are read before the planned duration/sample, or the team wants to ship to 100% immediately on a win **then** decisions rest on noisy partial data and may not scale (Google "fade-in links" wall of wrath) **because** early results reverse and small-sample wins don't generalize — **fix:** wait for the predetermined sample/run-length; ramp 1%→5%→25%→50% with a holdback group.
- **If** an experiment wins only for new users **then** suspect a novelty effect **because** people perform "better" out of excitement — **fix:** run long enough for novelty to wear off, then re-check.
- **If** a surprising/large result appears **then** replicate before acting **because** ~1 in 20 significant results at p=0.05 is a false positive (error compounds ~+5% per extra treatment) — **fix:** replicate; run fewer treatments or correct for multiple comparisons.
- **If** the chosen metric is holistic/insensitive (NPS) but the change is small (button copy) **then** you'll see "no change" despite a real effect **because** the metric lacks sensitivity — **fix:** pick a sensitive proxy metric capable of reflecting the change.
- **If** a flat (null) result appears **then** it's either underpowered OR a genuinely weak hypothesis **because** you can't yet distinguish them — **fix:** check sample/power, run-length, cell differentiation, and secondary metrics before concluding (~80% of tests fail to move predicted metrics).
- **If** the design makes the measured feature huge/prominent **then** the design, not the feature's value, drives the result **because** prominence inflates engagement — **fix:** design for overall balance, not to "win" a single test.
- **If** the team split-tests global navigation / stable core flows **then** you erode trust and habit **because** consistency is what users rely on — **fix:** restrict experiments to places users expect variation (SEM landing pages for new users).
- **If** an experiment manipulates socially-relevant info (compatibility scores, emotional content) **then** it crosses into deception/social manipulation **because** it can harm users beyond the interface (Facebook mood study, OKCupid) — **fix:** apply consent/IRB-style review; restrict to surface feature/flow testing.
## Web analytics interpretation

- **If** a page's bounce rate is high vs. *similar* pages, or % exit is high mid-process (middle of checkout) **then** entering users are unsatisfied / abandoning **because** of content mismatch, inscrutable design, or a friction step — **fix:** align content/labels to inbound intent; redesign that step; instrument an on-page success action to separate "satisfied & left" from "frustrated & left."
- **If** pageviews exceed unique pageviews by ~40–50%+ (pogo-sticking), or time-on-page is very low with a high revisit ratio **then** users are flailing **because** of confusing labels, duplicate links, or poor information scent — **fix:** clarify nav labels and orientation cues; improve search relevance (unless it's a legitimate hub).
- **If** a funnel shows a large drop between two specific steps **then** that step is the friction point **because** users abandon/divert there — **fix:** redesign that step; instrument funnels so per-step drop-off is visible; examine where abandoners exit to.
- **If** a site-wide average (bounce, conversion, time) judges a multi-audience site **then** it masks opposing group behaviors **because** target and non-target users are blended — **fix:** segment by persona/intent/source and report per-segment.
- **If** a persona-based segment captures only a tiny fraction of users **then** it's over-specified **because** persona narratives include incidental detail (exact time/device/keyword) — **fix:** generalize back to the underlying research; drop incidental traits.
- **If** a goal/KPI is set on a mid-page link or an engagement threshold (time/page-depth) **then** it counts accidental/ambiguous behavior or is a vanity metric **because** there's no proof of intent — **fix:** anchor goals to pages/actions reachable only deliberately (confirmation pages, deliberate forms).
- **If** a carousel/promo gets near-zero click-through (~2.3% of next pageviews), or an embedded video shows ~11s engagement **then** it lacks a clear CTA/context or doesn't match interest **because** users don't grasp its purpose — **fix:** add explicit CTA + sale/new indicator; reconsider whether it belongs; use event tracking for real engagement.
- **If** drop-off/abandon rate conflates quitting the *study* with quitting the *task*, or an "average" metric is reported without segment breakdowns **then** the metric is misleading **because** the mean hides distribution and tool conflation inflates it — **fix:** verify the tool distinguishes the two; report averages alongside segments.
## Prioritization, fixes & speed

- **If** fewer than ~70% of representative users complete a task **then** it's problematic and flags as vulnerable **because** 70% is a reasonable mid-iteration bar (target 95%) — **fix:** find the source of error and remediate; re-test.
- **If** a usability issue is seen by only one participant or is cosmetic **then** don't prioritize it over real obstacles **because** you may be chasing a fluke — **fix:** rate every issue on BOTH severity and frequency (criticality) before fixing.
- **If** a serious problem is deferred to "the upcoming redesign / Project Overlord" **then** it keeps hurting users **because** redesigns slip, get scrapped, or arrive late — **fix:** ship the smallest change now that smooths it for most people.
- **If** the team restates an *observed* nuisance as a "core architectural problem with no easy fix" **then** suspect cognitive dissonance ("if it were easy we'd have fixed it") **because** serious problems often do have simple mitigations — **fix:** find a stopgap that reduces impact now; tweak before redesigning.
- **If** the fix instinct is to add more text/instructions/color **then** you may be worsening an already-noisy page **because** the real problem is often too much already there — **fix:** question the addition; remove distractions instead.
- **If** a page load exceeds ~3–5 seconds **then** users flip from "acceptable" to "unacceptable" and physiological stress rises **because** the crossover sits between 3 and 5s — **fix:** keep key-page load times under that threshold.
## Reporting actionability

- **If** an issue is described abstractly ("the mental model doesn't match the user's") with no behavior cited **then** it's not actionable **because** it gives designers nothing to fix — **fix:** state behavior + interpretation + concrete example ("users jumped between top-nav sections trying to find X").
- **If** research findings read as "users need it to be easy to clean" **then** internal customers can't act **because** they aren't specified at engineering/design level — **fix:** translate to actionable specs ("elastomeric surfaces; joins ≤1mm gap").
- **If** a justification uses red-flag phrases ("Everyone does it," "It's better this way," "Studies show…", "our tests verify…") **then** an unexamined assumption or pseudo-science stands in for evidence **because** these mask the absence of real reasoning — **fix:** ask "Who is *everyone*? Who benefits?" and demand the actual reasoning/source.
- **If** the team rushes to implement off the topline / early findings **then** premature decisions are made on unprocessed data **because** early impressions aren't formal analysis — **fix:** label the topline "early impressions, not formal analysis"; show the calendar of what comes next.
- **If** a design decision rests only on analytics/satisfaction trending up, with no account of *why* **then** it may only show humans adapting/working around a flawed design **because** numbers show what, not reasoning — **fix:** validate with intent-level listening / generative research.
